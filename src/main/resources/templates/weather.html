Weather · providers, allowance and the anchor cache

Weather is fetched on demand for a point, never polled on a schedule. Every answer is served from an anchor if one is within 15 km reach and no older than 30m; only a miss reaches an upstream, and the upstreams are tried in the configured order until one is inside its free allowance. Reach is measured in three dimensions: at a vertical weight of 67, roughly 224 m of height difference costs as much as the whole reach, so the Hills are not served from the plains. The vertical term is off (weather.cache.vertical-weight: 0), so matching is horizontal only and an anchor across an escarpment can still answer for the valley below it.

The governor moves the reuse windows from how fast today's allowance is going, and it is insurance rather than a daily tuner. Open-Meteo publishes ten thousand units a day and a busy day spends a few hundred, so everything rests at its tightest setting essentially always. It earns its keep on the day Open-Meteo is down and Google's three hundred guarded calls are carrying the load, which is exactly the day nobody has time to edit a configuration file.

Anchors

A table of coordinates cannot show whether 15 km is the right reach. GET /api/weather/coverage.geojson draws the same anchors as four concentric outlines over the ground they cover: overlapping circles mean the radius is larger than it needs to be, gaps between them mean it is too small. The renderer for it is static/js/weather-map.js, carried across whole and waiting for a map page.

Drought cells

A soil moisture deficit integrated from 365 days of daily rain and maximum temperature. One cell serves 50 km for a whole day, because the quantity moves that slowly and deriving one costs a year of data.
